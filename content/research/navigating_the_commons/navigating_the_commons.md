##### Abstract

Inefficiencies in the commons arise not only from resource use by existing participants but also from their capacity investment and the entry of new firms. This paper develops a model of firm dynamics with common-pool externalities and applies it to the American whaling industry (1804--1909), an unregulated global commons. During the industry's growth, about 45 percent of the market price of a whale was the external cost (shadow value), two-thirds of which came from entry, exit, and investment. Compared with the first-best allocation, firms built 3.6 times the vessel capacity, dissipating 24 percent of welfare. Better harvesting technology or longer-lasting demand lowers welfare under open access, while improving welfare under the first best once externalities are properly priced.
